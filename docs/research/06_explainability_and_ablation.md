# 06. Explainability and Feature Ablation

## 1. Introduction

Machine learning models can identify complex relationships between clinical measurements and patient outcomes. However, understanding the factors influencing their predictions is important, particularly in healthcare-related applications.

This research uses SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to investigate the contribution of individual features to the predictions generated by the Original XGBoost model.

Feature ablation experiments were also conducted to evaluate the contribution of selected contextual features, particularly ICU Length of Stay (ICULOS) and Hospital Admission Time (HospAdmTime).

Together, these experiments provide complementary insights into model behavior.

## 2. SHAP Explainability

### 2.1 Overview

SHAP is an explainability technique based on Shapley values from cooperative game theory.

It estimates the contribution of individual input features to a model prediction.

In this research, SHAP was applied to the trained Original XGBoost model to investigate the influence of physiological, demographic, temporal, and contextual features.

### 2.2 Experimental Setup

The SHAP analysis used:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Model | Original XGBoost |
| Total input features | 48 |
| Test samples analyzed | 1,000 |
| SHAP output shape | (1000, 48) |

The analysis was performed using the trained model and its corresponding preprocessing components.

The resulting SHAP values were used to calculate mean absolute SHAP importance for each feature.

### 2.3 Global Feature Importance

Mean absolute SHAP values were calculated to rank features according to their overall contribution to model predictions.

### 2.4 Interpretation of SHAP Results

ICULOS had the highest mean absolute SHAP value, followed by HospAdmTime and Age.

This indicates that ICU timing information and patient context contributed substantially to the predictions made by the Original XGBoost model.

Several physiological and temporal features also appeared among the most influential features, including:

- Six-hour temperature mean.
- Current temperature.
- Six-hour respiratory rate mean.
- Oxygen saturation variability.
- Modified Shock Index.
- Six-hour MAP and SBP means.

The presence of rolling statistical features among the highly ranked variables suggests that the model uses information beyond individual physiological measurements.

However, mean absolute SHAP values measure the magnitude of feature contributions. They do not indicate whether a feature increases or decreases the predicted probability, establish causality, or prove that a feature is clinically important.

## 4. Feature Ablation Analysis

### 4.1 Objective

Feature ablation experiments were conducted to investigate the influence of ICU and hospital timing information on the predictive performance of the Original XGBoost model.

The analysis focused on two features:

- ICULOS
- HospAdmTime

These features were selected for investigation because they ranked highly in the SHAP feature importance analysis.

### 4.2 Experimental Configurations

Four XGBoost configurations were evaluated:

1. Original XGBoost using all 48 features.
2. XGBoost without ICULOS.
3. XGBoost without HospAdmTime.
4. XGBoost without both ICULOS and HospAdmTime.

The modified configurations were trained separately and evaluated on the same common test population.

### 4.3 Ablation Results

| Configuration | AUROC | AUPRC | Precision | Recall | F1-Score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original XGBoost | 0.7912 | 0.0994 | 0.1413 | 0.3377 | 0.1993 |
| Without ICULOS | 0.7167 | 0.0613 | 0.0779 | 0.2285 | 0.1162 |
| Without HospAdmTime | 0.7910 | 0.1035 | 0.1524 | 0.3138 | 0.2051 |
| Without both | 0.7055 | 0.0621 | 0.0827 | 0.2315 | 0.1218 |

### 4.4 Effect of Removing ICULOS

Removing ICULOS caused a substantial decrease in predictive performance.

The AUROC decreased from 0.7912 to 0.7167.

The AUPRC decreased from 0.0994 to 0.0613.

The F1-score also decreased from 0.1993 to 0.1162.

These results indicate that ICULOS provides important predictive information to the Original XGBoost model.

### 4.5 Effect of Removing HospAdmTime

Removing HospAdmTime alone produced relatively little change in AUROC.

The AUROC changed from 0.7912 to 0.7910.

AUPRC increased from 0.0994 to 0.1035, while F1-score increased from 0.1993 to 0.2051.

This suggests that HospAdmTime did not contribute as strongly to overall predictive performance as ICULOS in these experiments.

### 4.6 Effect of Removing Both Features

When both ICULOS and HospAdmTime were removed, the AUROC decreased to 0.7055 and AUPRC decreased to 0.0621.

This configuration produced results identical to the Clinical-only XGBoost configuration in the common test comparison.

The findings reinforce the importance of investigating ICU timing information when evaluating the model.

## 5. Combined Interpretation

The SHAP and feature ablation experiments provide complementary evidence about the model's use of input features.

SHAP identified ICULOS as the most influential feature, followed by HospAdmTime and Age.

The ablation experiment showed that removing ICULOS resulted in a substantial reduction in predictive performance, whereas removing HospAdmTime alone produced relatively little change.

These findings suggest that ICU length of stay is an important predictive variable in the evaluated model.

However, the experiments do not establish why the model relies on ICULOS or whether its contribution reflects clinically meaningful deterioration patterns, patient observation timing, or other temporal characteristics of the dataset.

Further investigation is required to determine whether the use of ICU timing variables introduces potential information leakage or limits the model's generalizability.

## 6. Limitations

The explainability and ablation experiments have several limitations:

1. SHAP analysis was performed on a sample of 1,000 test observations rather than the entire test population.
2. SHAP importance measures model contribution, not causality.
3. Feature ablation evaluates the effect of removing selected variables but does not independently establish their clinical relevance.
4. The influence of ICULOS requires further investigation to understand its relationship with prediction timing and potential information leakage.
5. The explanations describe the behavior of the fitted Original XGBoost model and should not automatically be generalized to the other model configurations.

## 7. Summary

SHAP analysis identified ICULOS, HospAdmTime, and Age as the most influential features in the Original XGBoost model.

Feature ablation experiments demonstrated that removing ICULOS substantially reduced predictive performance, while removing HospAdmTime alone had a limited effect.

These findings highlight the importance of temporal and contextual information in the evaluated model and motivate further investigation into the role of ICU timing features in early sepsis prediction.
